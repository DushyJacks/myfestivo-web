## VOICE & BRAND TONE

### Overall Tone
**"Professional Helpful"** — expert but not arrogant, useful but not salesy

- **Smart, not jargon-heavy.** Use clear language. Explain terms.
- **Conversational but structured.** Use headers, lists, white space.
- **Encouraging, not patronizing.** Treat readers as capable.
- **Honest about limitations.** Admit when we don't know. Admit when others are better at something.

### How We Talk About MyFestivo
- **Helpful solution**, not the only solution
- **Designed for college events**, not generic positioning
- **Fee-transparent**: mention savings compared to competitors where relevant
- **User-first not feature-first**: how it helps, not just what it has
- **Soft recommendations**: "Good for..." not "superior to..."

Example:
```
GOOD: "MyFestivo is great for college events where you need both payments and coordination. If you're just hosting free club meetings, DoorList or iCommunify might be simpler."

BAD: "MyFestivo is the best college event platform. Other platforms are outdated."
```

## LANGUAGE & WRITING RULES

### Formatting & Structure
- **Use lots of white space.** Don't write dense paragraphs.
- **Break content into short sections.** Math: ~300-400 words per section max.
- **Use H2 and H3 headers liberally.** Help readers scan and search.
- **Bullet lists are your friend.** Use them for tips, steps, comparisons.
- **Bold key phrases.** Not entire sentences, just key terms readers might skim for.

### Vocabulary
- **Target audience**: College students, event coordinators, student admins
- **Avoid**: Corporate jargon, buzzwords, hype language
- **Use**: Concrete nouns, active voice, simple words
- **Specificity**: "200-person hackathon" not "large event"

Example replacements:
```
INSTEAD OF          → USE
leverage            → use
synergize           → work together
utilize             → use
onboarding          → getting started
stakeholders        → team members, students
best practice       → what works, proven method
```

### Active vs Passive Voice
GOOD: "You can check in 500 students in 15 minutes with QR codes."
BAD: "Check-in can be performed in 15 minutes with QR codes."

## E-E-A-T MANDATORY SIGNALS

### EVERY blog post MUST include at least ONE of these:

1. **Experience** — "In our work with X college events, we learned..."
2. **Expertise** — "After running 50+ successful events, here's what we know..."
3. **Testimonial/Quote** — "As one student organizer told us, 'WhatsApp chaos is real.'"
4. **Metric/Data** — "Analysis of 1000+ college events shows that..."
5. **Review Link** — Link to MyFestivo reviews on G2, Trustpilot, etc. (once available)

### Where to Inject
- **Introduction**: Establish credibility early
- **Main section**: In a key claim or insight
- **Conclusion**: Reinforce trust before CTA

## COMPETITOR MENTIONS

### Rules
1. **Always mention competitor strengths first**
2. **Then mention trade-offs** (not just weaknesses)
3. **Link to their website** (fair, transparent)
4. **Cite from competitor_analysis.yaml** for confidence

Example:
```
✓ GOOD:
"iCommunify is great for US campus clubs — their RSVP calendar integration and attendance tracking are solid. The main trade-off is that they're still building out payment processing, which MyFestivo has fully integrated."

✗ BAD:
"iCommunify is incomplete compared to MyFestivo."
```

## SERP INTENT MATCHING

Before writing, confirm your blog matches the SERP intent you're targeting:

| If SERP shows... | Write a... | NOT a... |
|---|---|---|
| All product pages | product review or comparison | pure how-to guide |
| All how-to guides | comprehensive how-to | comparison |
| Mix of guides + tools | how-to with embedded tool tips | pure guide |
| All listicles | listicle or comparison | single deep-dive |
| All news/blogs | thought leadership or opinion | generic guide |

## COMMON PITFALLS TO AVOID

### Avoid: Saying "Another Version of What Exists"
❌ BAD: "Here's a comparison of college event platforms" (10 already exist)
✅ GOOD: "My analysis of 6 college event platforms and honest trade-offs" (adds perspective and honesty)

### Avoid: Fabricated Facts
❌ BAD: "Most college events use WhatsApp groups" (you don't have data to back this)
✅ GOOD: "Many college organizers tell us they use WhatsApp because..." (from our experience)

### Avoid: Burying Your Unique Angle
❌ BAD: Generic pillar that could be written by anyone
✅ GOOD: "Here's why WhatsApp is the real enemy of college events and how to escape it" (take a position)

### Avoid: Salesy Language
❌ BAD: "You NEED MyFestivo to manage events properly."
✅ GOOD: "If you're running large events with multiple teams, a dedicated platform helps..."

### Avoid: Missing Internal Links
❌ BAD: Post about payments with no link to financial dashboard blog
✅ GOOD: Link to supporting content (helps readers, helps SEO)

### Avoid: Skimming Over E-E-A-T
❌ BAD: No credibility signals anywhere
✅ GOOD: "Based on helping 50+ college events..." or DushyJacks insight

## FORMATTING CHECKLIST

Before publishing, check:
- [ ] Title ≤ 60 chars, includes primary keyword
- [ ] Description ≤ 160 chars, click-worthy
- [ ] Slug ≤ 5 words, kebab-case
- [ ] H2 headers: one per 500 words (approx)
- [ ] Bold key terms (not full sentences)
- [ ] Bullet lists where appropriate
- [ ] At least 4-6 internal links
- [ ] 1-2 external links to authority
- [ ] At least 1 E-E-A-T signal
- [ ] One soft CTA at end (never multiple)
- [ ] Images with alt text (alt text ≤ 125 chars)
- [ ] FAQ section included
- [ ] No superlatives without proof ("best" only if earned)

## EXAMPLE GOOD OPENING

```
One of the hardest parts of organizing a college event isn't the event itself—it's the chaos before it. You've got 500 people RSVPing on WhatsApp, 10 coordinators in a group chat arguing about tasks, payment tracking in an Excel sheet that nobody updated, and registration "confirmed" via email that half the students missed.

We've seen this exact situation at 50+ college events. And we've found that the real problem isn't your team or your event—it's your tools. You're using platforms built for corporate conferences (Eventbrite) or generic club management (iCommunify) when you actually need something made specifically for students running events.

This guide walks through the exact system we recommend and how it fixes each of these pain points.
```

**Why this works:**
- Opens with relatable problem (not generic intro)
- Uses "we" and "you" to engage
- Cites experience credibility
- Acknowledges other solutions exist (honest)
- Promises specific value
